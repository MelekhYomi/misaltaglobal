From bean to butter: how cocoa processing adds value at home

Mr HillaryaContributor · October 2026

Processing cocoa into butter and powder near where it grows keeps more value, jobs and skills in the local economy.

Much of the cocoa grown in West Africa leaves the region as raw beans, and most of the value is added elsewhere. Processing locally changes who benefits.

The journey starts with good beans: properly fermented and dried cocoa gives better flavour and higher yields of butter and powder. Beans are then cleaned, roasted and broken into nibs.

The nibs are ground into cocoa liquor, which is pressed to separate cocoa butter from the solids. The solids are milled into cocoa powder. Butter and powder are sold to chocolate, food, beverage and cosmetics makers.

Each step needs reliable power, clean facilities, trained people and quality control, which is why processing plants work best when they are planned together with supply, storage and energy.

Misalta Agro Processing is being developed in Taraba State to do exactly this: turn local cocoa into higher-value products close to where it is grown.
