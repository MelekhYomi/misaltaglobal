Solar and mini-grids: powering agro-processing in rural Nigeria

Samaila BahFounder & Chief Executive Officer · October 2026

Reliable power decides whether produce is processed where it grows or shipped away raw. Solar, storage and mini-grids are changing that equation.

Processing turns raw produce into higher-value products, but every dryer, grinder, press and cold room needs dependable electricity. In much of rural Nigeria the grid is far away or unreliable, so processors either run diesel generators at high cost or do not process at all.

Solar power changes the starting point. A solar array with battery storage can carry daytime processing loads, and a hybrid system with a small generator covers peak demand and cloudy days. Because the fuel is free, running costs become far more predictable than with diesel.

Mini-grids take the idea further: one system serves a processing site and the community around it. The processor becomes the anchor customer that makes the project bankable, and nearby homes and businesses gain power they could not get before.

There are trade-offs. Solar projects need upfront capital, careful sizing and good maintenance, and they must be permitted and certified by the right regulators. Getting the design right at the start avoids systems that are too small for the work or too large to pay back.

For agribusiness, the prize is simple: process close to the farm, reduce losses and keep more of the value in the communities where crops are grown. That is why energy sits alongside processing in the Misalta group.
