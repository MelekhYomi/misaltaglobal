Cutting post-harvest losses: why storage matters

Mr HillaryContributor · October 2026

A large share of what farmers grow is lost after harvest. Drying, clean storage and aggregation keep more of it, and more of its value.

Harvest is not the finish line. Between the field and the buyer, produce can be lost to moisture, pests, mould and poor handling, and every loss is income the farmer worked for and never receives.

Drying is the first line of defence. Grains and cocoa stored with too much moisture develop mould and lose quality quickly. Proper drying surfaces and simple moisture checks protect both quantity and grade.

Clean, ventilated storage comes next. Raised floors, sealed bags and regular inspection keep pests and damp away. Where storage is shared, good records show who owns what and its condition.

Storage also changes when farmers sell. Prices are often lowest at harvest, when everyone sells at once. Produce that keeps well can be sold later, when prices recover, or used as collateral for finance.

This is the idea behind Misalta Agro Hubs: aggregation, drying and storage close to where crops are grown, so farmers keep more of their harvest and buyers get consistent quality.
