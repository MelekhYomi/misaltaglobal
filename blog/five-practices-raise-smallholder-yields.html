Five practices that raise smallholder yields

Mr HillaryContributor · October 2026

Better seed, healthier soil, timely planting, careful weeding and good records: small changes that add up to bigger harvests.

Most farms can produce more from the same land with practices that are well understood but not always applied. Here are five that consistently make a difference.

First, use certified seed suited to the area. Good seed germinates evenly, resists common diseases and responds to care. Second, feed the soil: test it where possible, add organic matter and apply fertiliser at the right time and rate rather than all at once.

Third, plant on time. Planting with the first reliable rains gives crops the full season, while late planting often costs more yield than any input can recover. Fourth, control weeds early, because young crops lose the most when they compete for water and nutrients.

Fifth, keep simple records of what was planted, when, what was applied and what was harvested. Records turn one season's lessons into the next season's plan and make it easier to access credit and buyers.

None of these practices is new, but together they can transform results. Misalta Farms works with landowners and cooperatives to apply them at scale.
